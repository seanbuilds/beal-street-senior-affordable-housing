## 3. Detailed Transcripts & Timestamps of Critical Hearings

### A. February 26, 2019 Selectmen Meeting (`iSOkQlHX-5s`)
- **Key Timestamp Range:** `[00:00:01] - [00:07:58]`
- **Official Roster:** Paul Healey (Chairman), Karen Johnson (Selectman), Mary Power (Selectman), Susan Murphy (Town Counsel).
- **Core Excerpt:**
  > **[00:00:01] Chair Paul Healey:** "Okay, good evening ladies and gentlemen. At this time we are returning to open session after having voted previously to go into executive session to discuss the strategy with respect to potential litigation... entertain discussion with respect to the issue of 100 Beal Street in Hingham. We have with us tonight our counsel for the Town, attorney Susan Murphy. Please join us at the table and just briefly give us a backdrop of the issue before us tonight."
  > 
  > **[00:00:50] Town Counsel Susan Murphy:** "In 2001, the Board of Selectmen and the then board of the Housing Authority entered into a Memorandum of Understanding which was very brief—it's a one-page agreement pursuant to which the town agreed to pay for the costs of demolition and cleanup of two old structures on approximately 15 acres of land owned by the Housing Authority on Beal Street. These structures were buildings similar to the ones at Bare Cove Park in that area that had become dilapidated and had some hazardous materials in them, such as asbestos... An agreement was struck wherein consideration for the town paying the cost of demolition and remediation, that the Housing Authority would transfer eight of the fifteen-acre parcel that it owned on Beal Street... The Town did the work... For reasons which are not fully known, there was a gap in time between that work happening and before there being a realization that no deed had ever been recorded... brought to the Housing Authority's attention through multiple meetings... At the most recent meeting... a vote was taken as to whether or not the Housing Authority would follow through on its commitment to execute a deed... and the Housing Authority voted three votes against and only two votes in favor, and therefore the motion failed."
  > 
  > **[00:04:25] Selectman Karen Johnson:** "Taxpayer dollars were spent to demolish the buildings on Housing Authority property... We have reached an impasse... That's why as liaison to the Housing Authority I requested this executive session tonight so that I could fully brief you on the potential for litigation..."
  > 
  > **[00:07:49] Chair Paul Healey:** "Hearing none, I'll entertain a motion to file litigation regarding the enforcement of agreement as to 100 Beal Street in Hingham, Mass."
  > **Selectman Karen Johnson:** "Second."
  > **Chair Paul Healey:** "All in favor? Aye. (3–0 unanimous)."

### B. January 30, 2018 Selectmen Meeting (`KfiJSICX-mo`)
- **Key Timestamp Range:** `[02:09:40] - [02:16:00]`
- **Official Roster:** Paul Healey (Chairman), Karen Johnson (Selectman), Mary Power (Selectman), Tom Mayo (Town Administrator).
- **Core Excerpt:**
  > **[02:09:40] Chair Paul Healey:** "The last item on our agenda, I'm going to turn it over to Karen, is an update with respect to the Hingham Housing Authority and a discussion about 100 Beal Street."
  > 
  > **[02:09:51] Selectman Karen Johnson:** "Shortly after I took office in May, Tom Mayo, Ted Alexiades, and I met with Davalene Cooper, who was then the chair of the Hingham Housing Authority, to talk about the transfer of about 8 acres of land on Beal Street... Back about 17 or 18 years ago there were a bunch of bunkers... around Bare Cove Park in this Beal Street vicinity... The Housing Authority didn't have cash to pay for that, so the Housing Authority said: 'Well, we'll give you some of this land that we own on Beal Street because we don't have any need for it, and a certain part of that acreage in any event is wetlands'... The Town entered into a contract in 2001... The deed was never executed... In 2016/2017, in the midst of doing diligence around parcels owned by the Selectmen on Beal Street prior to the Alliance project, through Town Counsel Susan Murphy we discovered this eight acres never came to the Town... We provided a full diligence package... The Housing Authority instead took a vote not to transfer this land back to the town, a 3 to 1 vote... We are now in a situation where the Housing Authority has a parcel of land that really belongs to the Town."

### D. November 4, 2024 Conservation Commission Hearing (DEP No. 034-1509)
- **Meeting Format:** Remote Public Hearing via Zoom (Meeting ID: `825 1522 3257`).
- **Official Roster:** Carolyn Nielsen (Chair), Bob Mosher, Tom Roby, Nina Villanova, Laurie Freeman; Shannon Palmer (Conservation Officer); Christopher Lucas, PWS/PSS (Lucas Environmental, LLC).
- **Proceedings Summary & Excerpts from Official Hearing Records:**
  > **Abbreviated Notice of Resource Area Delineation (ANRAD) – 100 Beal Street, MassDEP No. 034-1509:**
  > • Abutters duly notified by Certificates of Mailing documented in Affidavit of Service.
  > • **Presentation by Christopher Lucas (Lucas Environmental):** Represented applicant team and explained request for boundary confirmation. Emphasized that no construction work was proposed under the ANRAD. Noted the majority of the 8.6-acre site is wet and outlined key natural features:
  >   - Three distinct systems of Bordering Vegetated Wetland (BVW).
  >   - Two systems of Isolated Vegetative Wetlands (IVW).
  >   - A confirmed vernal pool located at the corner of the parcel.
  > • **Conservation Officer Shannon Palmer Review:** Confirmed field inspections and alignment with staff recommendations and the 4-sheet plan by Control Point Associates, Inc. (dated September 9, 2024).
  > • **Public Input:** Chair Nielsen opened the hearing to members of the public; no abutter or citizen spoke.
  > • **Formal Vote:** Commissioner Laurie Freeman moved, seconded by Commissioner Bob Mosher, to close the public hearing and issue an Order of Resource Area Delineation (ORAD) for 100 Beal Street (DEP No. 034-1509) confirming all resource boundaries, isolated wetland classifications, and vernal pool designations. Roll call vote passed **5–0** (Carolyn Nielsen, Bob Mosher, Tom Roby, Nina Villanova, Laurie Freeman in favor). Formal ORAD issued on **November 7, 2024**.
