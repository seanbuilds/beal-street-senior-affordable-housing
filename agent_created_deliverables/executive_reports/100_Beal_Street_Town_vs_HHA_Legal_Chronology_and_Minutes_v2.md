## 1. Evidentiary Categorization Framework

To prevent the conflation of documented facts with subjective interpretations or unresolved legal disputes, every item in this record is evaluated against three distinct evidentiary categories:

1. **Verified Documentary Facts:** Objective assertions directly quoted or summarized from authenticated primary records (recorded Registry deeds, signed municipal minutes, certified Town Meeting election returns, published warrants, and official state agency filings).
2. **Factual Inferences (`[Factual Inference]`):** Deductions regarding timing, context, or procedural posture that are logically supported by the record sequence but lack an express, verbatim confirmation in a single standalone document.
3. **Open or Disputed Legal Questions (`[Open / Disputed Legal Question]`):** Substantive legal determinations regarding property rights, reverter expiration, condition compliance, or regulatory jurisdiction that remain contested or require formal judicial or title counsel adjudication.

## 2. Statutory Foundations & The 1989 Fee Conveyance

### 2.1 The Recorded Fee Deed (Book 09097, Pages 158–161)
* **Date of Execution:** March 7, 1989  
* **Date of Recording:** April 21, 1989 at 11:58 AM  
* **Recording Citation:** Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, **Book 09097, Pages 158–161** (Instrument #29758; 4 pages)  
* **Grantor:** Town of Hingham, acting by and through its Board of Selectmen (Alexander A. Randall, Chairman; John P. Ryan; William J. Salisbury) pursuant to authority under May 1987 Annual Town Meeting Warrant Article 53.  
* **Grantee:** Hingham Housing Authority (HHA), a public body politic and corporate organized pursuant to M.G.L. c. 121B.  
* **Consideration:** $45,751.00 paid into the Town Treasury.  
* **Legal Description:** School Tract II, containing **15.014 net acres**, as shown on Perkins Engineering Plan (Plan Book 17, Page 508).

### 2.2 Verbatim Deed Condition & Reverter Clause (Book 09097, Page 160)
The deed conveyed fee ownership subject to an express educational restriction and a thirty-year right of entry:

> *"This conveyance is made upon the express condition that the property will be used only for a residential educational facility for emotionally disturbed adolescents operated through the Hingham Housing Authority by the South Shore Educational Collaborative or similar entity; or, if required pursuant to M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34, by the Massachusetts Executive Office of Communities and Development.*  
>  
> *In the event that a breach of the above condition should occur after December 17, 2003, all right, title and interest in and to the above described property shall revert to and become the property of the Town of Hingham which shall have the immediate right of entry thereon, **provided, however, that said right of entry shall terminate after thirty years from the date hereof as provided in Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 184A, Section 3.**"*  
> *(Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, Book 09097, Page 160).*

### 2.3 Historical Statutory Context & Legal Considerations

* **The Historical Statute vs. Modern Recodifications:**  
  The 1989 deed references "Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 184A, Section 3." At the time of deed drafting and execution (March 7, 1989), Chapter 184A contained Massachusetts statutory provisions relating to the rule against perpetuities and restrictions on land.  
  * Shortly thereafter, the Massachusetts Legislature enacted Chapter 668 of the Acts of 1989 (effective June 30, 1989), adopting the Uniform Statutory Rule Against Perpetuities and restructuring Chapter 184A.  
  * Subsequently, Chapter 521 of the Acts of 2008 repealed Sections 1 through 4 of Chapter 184A in conjunction with the Massachusetts Uniform Probate Code.  
  * Concurrently, **M.G.L. c. 184, § 23** sets forth a general statutory rule that conditions or restrictions unlimited as to time shall be limited to the term of thirty years, subject to specific statutory definitions and exceptions.  
  * `[Open / Disputed Legal Question]`: The exact statutory basis, governing historical version, and legal effect of the thirty-year limitation clause require confirmation under the historical law applicable to the March 7, 1989 deed and a comprehensive Massachusetts title examination.

* **Factual Predicate of Use vs. Reverter Operation:**  
  * `[Verified Documentary Fact]`: Available municipal records reflect that the property was developed as an adolescent residential facility (operated by Bay State Community Services under contract with the Department of Children and Families [DCF]) funded via state Chapter 689 assistance.  
  * `[Open / Disputed Legal Question]`: Available records should be reviewed by title counsel to determine whether any breach notice, declaration of default, entry, litigation, or recorded enforcement instrument was formally issued or recorded prior to the asserted expiration date.

* **Fee Title Status:**  
  * `[Open / Disputed Legal Question]`: Rather than an unencumbered "fee simple absolute," HHA appears to be the record fee owner under the recorded deed, subject to a complete title examination and all recorded and statutory encumbrances (including state housing restrictions, wetlands orders, zoning permits, and potential unrecorded municipal claims).

* **Surveyor Title Abstract Observations (Control Point Associates ALTA Survey, 2024):**  
  * On Sheet 1 of the ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey prepared by Control Point Associates, Inc. (Attachment B of the HHA Developer RFP), Note 3 of Schedule B, Part II references the 1989 deed (Book 9097, Page 158) and contains the following title notation:  
    > *"NOTE: THE RIGHT OF REVERTER TO THE TOWN FOR BREACH OF THIS CONDITION EXPIRED ON DECEMBER 17, 2003, BUT THE RIGHT OF TERMINATION REMAINS UNTIL MARCH 7, 2029. THEREFORE NEED RELEASE OR CHANGE OF THIS RESTRICTION FROM THE TOWN OF HINGHAM."*  
  * `[Open / Disputed Legal Question]`: This surveyor note illustrates the divergent legal interpretations surrounding the deed conditions, highlighting that whether a formal municipal release is required remains an active title question among professionals.

## 10. Concluding Observations & Legal Review Recommendations

1. **Record Title Status:** The Hingham Housing Authority appears on the public land records as the fee owner of School Tract II pursuant to the 1989 deed (Book 09097, Page 158). However, title is subject to active recorded encumbrances, including the 2019 Notice of Statutory Transfer Restriction (Book 51379, Page 244) and the 2024 Order of Resource Area Delineation (Book 59527, Page 273).
2. **Deed Restriction Analysis:** The 1989 deed condition restricting use to an adolescent facility and its thirty-year reverter clause present nuanced questions of historical statutory interpretation under M.G.L. c. 184A, § 3, c. 184, § 23, and subsequent legislative amendments. While the thirty-year period elapsed on March 7, 2019, whether this automatically extinguished all municipal rights or whether an unrecorded release or dispute remains active requires formal title counsel examination.
3. **Intergovernmental Jurisdiction:** The interaction between municipal board votes (such as the Select Board's 2019 litigation authorization) and state public housing supervisory authority under M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34 demonstrates that public housing property dispositions are subject to rigorous Commonwealth statutory constraints that supersede local municipal arrangements.
4. **Procurement & Development Completion:** While the HHA has designated Peabody Properties / AHSC under M.G.L. c. 30B, final execution of a Land Disposition Agreement and a 99-year Ground Lease, along with full documentation of Chapter 30B procedural compliance and Zoning Board of Appeals Chapter 40B Comprehensive Permit approval, remains necessary before development rights are formally perfected.
