## 1. Project Overview & Executive Summary

The **Beal Street Senior Affordable Housing** development (commonly designated as **100 Beal Street** or **School Tract II**) represents a multi-decade municipal land-use and affordable housing initiative in the Town of Hingham, Massachusetts.

### Key Project Parameters:
- **Location:** 100 Beal Street, Hingham, MA 02043 (Assessors Map 58, Block 0, Lot 23).
- **Total Parcel Area:** 15.014 acres (comprising School Tract II, created on the Perkins Engineering July 1972 Plan Book 17, Page 508).
- **Developable Development Envelope:** Approximately 8.6 acres declared surplus/excess by the Hingham Housing Authority (HHA) under M.G.L. c. 30B.
- **Current Site Use:** Existing residential educational facility / adolescent group home (6 units of special needs group housing operated by Bay State Community Services under contract with the Massachusetts Department of Children and Families).
- **Proposed Building Program:** A 3-story residential facility stepped into woodland contours containing **68 units of deeply affordable senior rental housing** (designated for seniors age 55+ / 62+ with local municipal and veteran preferences), advancing from early 20–48 unit conceptual studies and the 60-unit target set in the 2025 Request for Proposals.
- **Lead Developer:** **Peabody Properties / Affordable Housing Services Corporation (AHSC)** (awarded August 12, 2025; Land Disposition and Development Agreement approved August 25, 2026).
- **Lead Civil Engineers & Landscape Architects:** **Weston & Sampson**, in coordination with **Lucas Environmental, LLC**, **Control Point Associates, Inc.**, and **Bohler Engineering, Inc.**
- **Regulatory Framework:** Comprehensive Permit pursuant to **M.G.L. c. 40B** before the Hingham Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA), required due to the underlying **Official and Open Space (OO)** zoning prohibiting multi-family dwellings.
- **Environmental Constraints:** Direct adjacency to the 484-acre **Bare Cove Park**; hydrologically connected to **Tucker's Swamp**, **Hockley Run**, and the tidal **Weymouth Back River Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC)**. Wetland boundaries formally established under MassDEP File No. **034-1509** (Order of Resource Area Delineation issued November 7, 2024).

## 4. Architectural & Civil Engineering Design (Weston & Sampson / Peabody)

Based on the ultra-high-resolution March 3, 2026 site plan update (`20260303-Beal-Street-Housing-Rendered-Plan-Update.jpg`) and architectural renderings:

- **Building Massing & Typology:**
  - 3-story modern residential building footprint specifically designed to step into the natural woodland topography.
  - Total unit capacity: 68 senior affordable rental apartments (advancing from earlier 50-unit and 60-unit conceptual iterations).
  - Dedicated common amenity spaces: central multi-purpose community hall, wellness suite, quiet reading rooms, administrative management office, and package reception.
- **Site Access & Circulation:**
  - Main vehicular ingress/egress from Beal Street through an improved, traffic-calmed curb cut.
  - Surface parking carefully arranged along the northern and interior facade, avoiding intrusion into the 50-foot wetland setback.
  - Full perimeter ADA-compliant paved multi-use walking loop connecting to outdoor recreation zones and Bare Cove Park paths.
- **Landscape Architecture & Stormwater:**
  - Low Impact Development (LID) bio-retention swales, rain gardens, and subterranean infiltration chambers engineered to protect the Weymouth Back River watershed.
  - Native pollinator gardens, sensory planting zones, shade trees to mitigate urban heat island effects, and a resident raised-bed garden patio.
  - Outdoor gathering terraces featuring shade pergolas and bench seating.

## 5. Summary of Regulatory & Permitting Trajectory

- **Early Feasibility (GM2 Associates / MHP):** Initial site assessment of 15 acres of HHA land; conceptualized 50 units with parking lot relocation.
- **RFP Award (August 2025):** Hingham Housing Authority Board of Commissioners formally voted to select Peabody Properties / AHSC as the designated developer.
- **Conservation Commission (Fall 2024 – 2026):** ANRAD filed under DEP File # 034-1509; Order of Resource Area Delineation (ORAD) issued confirming CPA / Lucas Environmental wetland boundary flagging.
- **Comprehensive Permitting (Chapter 40B):** Formal application pipeline for a friendly Comprehensive Permit under M.G.L. c. 40B with the Hingham Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) for 68 units of senior affordable housing.

## 3. Detailed Transcripts & Timestamps of Critical Hearings

### A. February 26, 2019 Selectmen Meeting (`iSOkQlHX-5s`)
- **Key Timestamp Range:** `[00:00:01] - [00:07:58]`
- **Official Roster:** Paul Healey (Chairman), Karen Johnson (Selectman), Mary Power (Selectman), Susan Murphy (Town Counsel).
- **Core Excerpt:**
  > **[00:00:01] Chair Paul Healey:** "Okay, good evening ladies and gentlemen. At this time we are returning to open session after having voted previously to go into executive session to discuss the strategy with respect to potential litigation... entertain discussion with respect to the issue of 100 Beal Street in Hingham. We have with us tonight our counsel for the Town, attorney Susan Murphy. Please join us at the table and just briefly give us a backdrop of the issue before us tonight."
  > 
  > **[00:00:50] Town Counsel Susan Murphy:** "In 2001, the Board of Selectmen and the then board of the Housing Authority entered into a Memorandum of Understanding which was very brief—it's a one-page agreement pursuant to which the town agreed to pay for the costs of demolition and cleanup of two old structures on approximately 15 acres of land owned by the Housing Authority on Beal Street. These structures were buildings similar to the ones at Bare Cove Park in that area that had become dilapidated and had some hazardous materials in them, such as asbestos... An agreement was struck wherein consideration for the town paying the cost of demolition and remediation, that the Housing Authority would transfer eight of the fifteen-acre parcel that it owned on Beal Street... The Town did the work... For reasons which are not fully known, there was a gap in time between that work happening and before there being a realization that no deed had ever been recorded... brought to the Housing Authority's attention through multiple meetings... At the most recent meeting... a vote was taken as to whether or not the Housing Authority would follow through on its commitment to execute a deed... and the Housing Authority voted three votes against and only two votes in favor, and therefore the motion failed."
  > 
  > **[00:04:25] Selectman Karen Johnson:** "Taxpayer dollars were spent to demolish the buildings on Housing Authority property... We have reached an impasse... That's why as liaison to the Housing Authority I requested this executive session tonight so that I could fully brief you on the potential for litigation..."
  > 
  > **[00:07:49] Chair Paul Healey:** "Hearing none, I'll entertain a motion to file litigation regarding the enforcement of agreement as to 100 Beal Street in Hingham, Mass."
  > **Selectman Karen Johnson:** "Second."
  > **Chair Paul Healey:** "All in favor? Aye. (3–0 unanimous)."
