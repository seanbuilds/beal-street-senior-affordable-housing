### Slide 2: Origins: The 1989 Town Conveyance & 30-Year Reverter
* **Slide Title:** March 1989: The Town Deeds School Tract II
* **Subtitle:** 15.014 Acres Conveyed for Adolescent Care with a 30-Year Reverter
* **Visual Composition:** High-resolution scan of the original 1989 Deed (Book 09097, Page 158). Callout of the express educational restriction on Page 160 and the 30-year right of entry clause.
* **On-Screen Highlights:**
  - Date: March 7, 1989 | Recorded April 21, 1989 (Plymouth Registry Book 09097, Page 158)
  - Grantor: Town of Hingham (Board of Selectmen) pursuant to 1987 ATM Article 53
  - Grantee: Hingham Housing Authority | Purchase Consideration: $45,751.00
  - Parcel Scope: Entire 15.014 Net Acres (School Tract II)
  - Deed Restriction: Property to be used *solely* for a residential facility for emotionally disturbed adolescents
  - Reverter Clause: Right of entry terminates after 30 years under M.G.L. c. 184A, § 3
* **Verbatim Spoken Narration (115 words):**
  > *"Our story begins in the late 1980s. Following the decommission of the Hingham Naval Ammunition Depot, the Town owned surplus federal land. In March 1989, the Selectmen deeded a fifteen-acre parcel known as School Tract II to the Hingham Housing Authority for forty-five thousand seven hundred fifty-one dollars. Importantly, the deed restricted the entire fifteen acres solely for an educational residential facility for troubled adolescents. However, the deed included an explicit time limit: it stated that if the adolescent use ceased, the Town retained a right of entry to take the property back, but that right of entry would terminate after thirty years under Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 184A, Section 3."*

### Slide 9: Summer 2019: State DHCD Supervisory Intervention

* **On-Screen Highlights:**
  - Governing Statute: Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B (Housing Authorities)
  - Legal Autonomy: Housing authorities are independent state corporations, not Town departments
  - Supervisory Regulator: Massachusetts DHCD (Associate Director Amy Stitely)
  - Statutory Bar: M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34 strictly forbids transferring public housing land without state approval
  - State Letters (April 19 & June 26, 2019): DHCD warns Town Counsel that transfer is unauthorized
* **Verbatim Spoken Narration (110 words):**
  > *"Under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B, local housing authorities are independent public corporations created by the Legislature to serve low-income residents. Because state Chapter 689 funding was used to build the adolescent home, the entire parcel is protected by state public housing covenants. Under Section 34 of Chapter 121B, no housing authority property may be sold, transferred, or leased without prior written approval from the Commonwealth. Massachusetts DHCD Associate Director Amy Stitely notified both the Housing Authority and Town Counsel that the proposed transfer violated state law and public housing policy, and would not be approved."*
