# April 1989: The Deed & The 30-Year Reverter
## Town Conveys 15.014 Acres of Surplus Military Land to HHA

### Recorded Fee Deed Facts
- **Execution Date:** March 7, 1989 | **Recording Date:** April 21, 1989
- **Registry Citation:** Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, Book 09097, Page 158 (Doc #29758)
- **Grantor:** Inhabitants of the Town of Hingham &rarr; **Grantee:** Hingham Housing Authority
- **Stated Consideration:** $45,751 for 15.014-Acre School Tract II

### The 30-Year Reverter Clause (Page 160)
- **Specific Use Restriction:** Dedicated solely for adolescent residential educational facility
- **Right of Re-Entry:** Town retained right to enter if adolescent care ceased
- **Statutory Limitation:** Deed expressly dictates re-entry terminates after thirty years pursuant to M.G.L. c. 184A, § 3
- **Expiration Deadline:** March 7, 2019 (Exactly 30 calendar years)

<!-- 
Speaker Notes:
Our story begins on April 21st, 1989. The Town of Hingham conveyed a fifteen-acre parcel of surplus Navy depot land known as School Tract II to the independent Hingham Housing Authority for forty-five thousand seven hundred fifty-one dollars. The deed carried a specific restriction: the land was to be used for a residential educational facility for troubled adolescents. Critically, page one hundred sixty stated that if that youth use ever ceased, the Town retained a right of re-entry. # 1990 to Present: Continuous Public Service
## The 12-Bed Adolescent Facility Operates Without Interruption

### Facility Operations & Compliance
- **Footprint:** 12-bed group home constructed on dedicated 2.0-acre parcel fronting Beal Street
- **Funding Program:** Commonwealth Chapter 689 specialized public housing funds
- **Operational Oversight:** State youth services contract and licensed child care provider
- **Continuous Public Service:** Over 35 consecutive years of unbroken operation

### Legal Impact on Town Re-Entry
- **Legal Condition Precedent:** Re-entry required complete cessation of adolescent residential use
- **Zero Default:** Facility never ceased operations or abandoned its mission
- **State Encumbrance:** Chapter 689 funding attached permanent state public housing covenants
- **Unbroken Title:** The Town never had lawful grounds to trigger re-entry during the 30-year window

# Summer 2019: The State Steps In
## Massachusetts DHCD Enforces Statutory Protections on Public Housing Land

### Independent Statutory Entity
- **Governing Authority:** Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B (Housing & Urban Renewal)
- **Corporate Separation:** Housing authorities are independent public bodies corporate, not Town departments
- **Jurisdiction:** Board of Selectmen has zero statutory authority to command housing assets
- **Dedicated Purpose:** Real estate must remain dedicated to low-income housing needs

### Statutory Bar: M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34
- **Supervisory Regulator:** Massachusetts DHCD (Associate Director Amy Stitely)
- **Regulatory Letters:** Issued April 19 & June 26, 2019 to HHA and Town Counsel
- **Statutory Prohibition:** Public housing land cannot be transferred without written state approval
- **State Ruling:** DHCD formally refuses to consent to municipal conveyance

<!-- 
Speaker Notes:
Town Hall's victory was short-lived. Under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B, local housing authorities are independent public corporations created by the Legislature, not municipal departments. Furthermore, because state public housing funding had been utilized at the Beal Street site, state law strictly prohibits any housing authority from selling or transferring public housing land without prior written approval from the Commonwealth. Massachusetts DHCD Associate Director Amy Stitely issued formal warnings to both the Housing Authority and Town Counsel, stating plainly that the proposed transfer violated state law and public housing covenants, and would not be approved.
