100 Beal Street: The 37-Year Journey

From Naval Munitions Bunkers to State-Protected Senior Affordable Housing (1989–2026)

Project Overview & Scope

  • Location: 100 Beal Street, Hingham, MA (School Tract II)
  • Record Property Owner: Hingham Housing Authority (Independent State Entity)
  • Proposed Development: 68-Unit Senior Affordable Rental Residence (Ages 62+)
  • Selected Developer: Peabody Properties / AHSC (99-Year Ground Lease)
  • Municipal Taxpayer Cost: Exactly $0 in Town Borrowing or Taxpayer Debt

Chronological Milestones

  • 1989: Town deeds 15 acres with 30-year adolescent care reverter
  • 2001: WWII naval munitions bunkers demolished; 8-acre Lot 2 created
  • 2006: Town Meeting 2/3 vote mandates senior affordable housing
  • 2019: Select Board 3–0 litigation vote; State DHCD statutory transfer block
  • 2021: Housing Authority votes 4–0 to permanently retain land for housing
  • 2026: Project fully active and permitted under long-term ground lease

Summer 2019: The State Steps In

Massachusetts DHCD Enforces Statutory Protections on Public Housing Land

Primary Historical Facts

  • Governing Authority: Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B (Housing & Urban Renewal)
  • Legal Status: Housing authorities are independent public bodies corporate, not Town departments
  • Jurisdiction: Board of Selectmen has zero statutory authority to seize or command housing assets
  • Public Purpose: Land dedicated to low-income housing cannot be alienated for general municipal use

Documentary & Legal Impact

  • Supervisory Regulator: Massachusetts DHCD (Associate Director Amy Stitely)
  • Statutory Bar (M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34): Public housing land cannot be transferred without written state approval
  • Regulatory Letters: Issued April 19 & June 26, 2019 to HHA and Town Counsel
  • State Decision: DHCD formally refuses to consent to municipal recapture
