The hearing was closed at the November 4, 2024 meeting. Staff reviewed recommended conditions.
Vote: Commissioner Freeman made a motion, seconded by Commissioner Mosher, to issue an Order of
Conditions for Union Street, DEP No. 034-1506, with Findings of Fact A through F and Special Conditions
23-30 as noted in the Staff Memo.
Findings of Fact
A. The Commission has found the project qualifies as a Limited Project under 310 CMR 10.53(i) “the
maintenance, repair and improvement (but not substantial enlargement except when necessary
to meet the Massachusetts Stream Crossing Standards) of structures, including dams and
reservoirs and appurtenant works to such dams and reservoirs, buildings, piers, towers, headwalls,
bridges, and culverts which existed on the effective date of April 1, 1983,” and 310 CMR 10.53(k):
“the routine maintenance and repair of road drainage structures including culverts and catch
basins, drainage easements, ditches, watercourses and artificial water conveyances to ensure flow
capacities which existed on the effective date of April 1, 1983.”
B. The Commission has found the culvert replacement project has been designed to meet the
Massachusetts Stream Crossing Standards and will improve conditions in the Aaron River for
movement of aquatic organisms and wildlife species.
C. The work described in the Notice of Intent is within Areas Subject to Protection under the
Wetlands Protection Act, M.G.L. Ch. 131, § 40, and will result in impacts to permanent impacts to
Bank, Bordering Vegetated Wetlands, BLSF, Land Under Waterbodies and Riverfront Area. As
demonstrated in the Performance Standards Analysis prepared by AECOM submitted with the
revised Notice of Intent dated October 29, 2024, the project has been designed to comply with the
standards set forth under 310 CMR 10.54, 10.55, 10.56, 10.57, and 10.58(5) and the work will not
have a significant or cumulative adverse effects on the resource areas.
D. The work described is within Areas Subject to Protection under the Town of Hingham Wetlands
Protection Bylaw (Article 22) and will result in permanent impacts to Bank, Bordering Vegetated
Wetlands, BLSF, Land Under Waterbodies, Riverfront Area and Buffer Zone and the Commission
finds the areas are significant to the following wetland values: protection of public or private water
supply, protection of groundwater, flood control, erosion and sedimentation control, storm damage
prevention including coastal storm flowage, prevention of water pollution, protection of fisheries,
protection of shellfish, protection of wildlife and wildlife habitat, protection of rare species habitat,
protection of recreation and open space, protection of aesthetics.
E. As demonstrated in the revised NOI Narrative prepared by AECOM dated October 29, 2024, the
project has been designed to comply with the performance standards set forth under the Hingham
Wetland Regulations (HWR) Sections 17.3, 19.1, 19.2, 19.3, 21.1, and will not result in an adverse
effect upon the wetland values of the resource areas. The project includes restoration of
temporarily disturbed areas and replication of BVW to mitigate for the permanent loss of wetlands
resulting from the culvert replacement. Additionally the proposed work has been found to comply
with the performance standards for work in the Buffer Zone, HWR Section 22.0(d).
F. With incorporation of the special conditions included in this Order, the Commission has determined
the project complies with the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act, M.G.L. Ch. 131 §40 and
Implementing Regulations, 310 CMR 10.00, and the Hingham Wetlands Protection Bylaw and
Wetland Regulations.
Recommended Special Conditions
23. Prior to commencement or work/at the pre-construction meeting referenced in Hingham Standard
Condition #3, the applicant shall submit a final construction sequence and dewatering plan as applicable
to the Conservation office.

24. Best Management Practices (BMPs) and procedures shall be implemented within jurisdictional areas in
accordance with the revised Notice of Intent Narrative (Section 5.1) and Sheet 6 of the approved
project plans prepared by AECOM dated revised October 29, 2024 including but not limited to:
sediment and erosion control; construction access; vehicle and equipment refueling and maintenance;
material stockpiling; dewatering; pollution control measures, and restoration of disturbed areas.
25. Construction period monitoring reports shall be submitted to the Commission on a weekly basis during
construction documenting compliance with the approved plans and this Order of Conditions. The
monitoring reports shall include at a minimum: status of work in wetland areas; status of erosion and
sediment controls and BMPs including any repairs; rainfall amounts; and comments.
26. The replication for Bordering Vegetated Wetland (BVW) shall be performed in accordance with
Section 6.0 of the NOI Narrative and the Wetland Replication Plan (Sheet 6) of the approved plans
prepared by AECOM dated revised October 29, 2024. The project will replicate 73 square feet of
BVW. The Commission reserves the right to require additional seeding/plantings to ensure
achievement of 75% cover of wetland plant species within two full growing seasons, as specified in
310 CMR 10.55 (4)(b).
27. Replication construction activities must be supervised by a qualified professional, who shall be a
professional with experience in wetland replication, wetland hydrology and a working knowledge of
botany. Such a person shall be retained by the applicant to supervise and monitor construction of
the wetland replication areas until the replication area meets the requirements of this Order of
Conditions.
28. Seasonal monitoring reports shall be prepared by the qualified professional for the wetland
replication area for two growing seasons following construction. The reports shall be submitted to
the Commission by December 1st following the growing season. Monitoring reports shall describe,
using narratives, plans, and color photographs, the physical characteristics of the wetland
replication areas with respect to stability, soil characteristics, survival of vegetation and plant
mortality, aerial extent and distribution, species diversity, invasive species, etc. If after two growing
seasons, the replication area has not successfully established the required 75% yield, a contingency
plan including extended monitoring shall be submitted to the Commission for approval.
29. Areas of temporary resource area disturbance (BVW, Riverfront Area, Bank) shall be restored to
their natural condition (pre-construction elevations and conditions) following construction.
Disturbed areas within BVW shall be reseeded with NE Wetland Mix and disturbed areas within the
Riverfront Area shall be seeded with NE Roadside Upland Seed Mix or equivalent as specified in
Section 4.2 of the NOI Narrative. Reporting on the restoration of BVW shall be included in the
seasonal monitoring reports referenced in condition #27 as applicable.
30. Upon completion of construction and prior to issuance of a Certificate of Compliance, the Applicant
shall submit to the Conservation Commission a final topographical As-Built Plan stamped by a
Professional Land Surveyor licensed in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts and a certification
letter stamped by a Professional Civil Engineer licensed in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts
stating that the site work has been built in “substantial compliance” with the plans approved by the
Conservation Commission and listing all deviations, as applicable. The letter and/or plan shall
document final compensatory storage volumes and certify that no fill occurred within Bordering
Land Subject to Flooding resulting in an incremental loss of flood storage volume. The As-Built Plan
shall include:
• All wetland resource area boundaries with associated buffer zones;
• Culvert dimensions and invert elevations at inlet and outlet;
• Bottom substrate within crossing;
• Wetland replication area.

discussion at the previous hearing was regarding the proposed house being constructed on a crawl
space foundation with flood vents as opposed to pilings and if the proposal met the requirements of the

meet the requirement for LSCSF as the residence will be smaller, flood resistant, and is not located in a

flood water to pass through. Ms. Palmer did voice concerns about the patio as it is proposed extremely
close to the wetland boundary. Ms. Palmer, at the Commission’s request sent a list of similar dwellings
that were previously approved in the AE zone. All examples found were on the harbor, some were in the
VE zone, and most were on pilings that were being replaced or increasing the size of the residence.

slab.
Mr. Tremblay advised that the garage is sloped toward the middle of the driveway and flow to its
previous pattern around the house to the salt marsh. The paved area in the rear that will be removed
will be a grassed area helping to slow down the water.

naturally revegetate could result in Phragmites. She asked if there was a plan to prevent this.

proposed but would be happy to comply with the Commissions requirements.

agreed.

reduce or remove the patio.

away from the salt marsh the paved driveway which is currently 6” away from the salt marsh is being
removed.
