Recommended Findings of Fact
A. The Commission has found the invasive species management project qualifies as an Ecological
Restoration Limited Project under 310 CMR 10.53(4)(e)(5) as the proposed activities will improve the
natural capacity of a Resource Area(s) to protect the interests identified in M.G.L. c. 131, § 40. The
proposal is for the removal of aquatic nuisance vegetation, Phragmites, to retard pond and lake
eutrophication and will also result in an improvement to habitat value and re-naturalization of the
riparian corridor.
B. The work described in the Ecological Restoration Notice of Intent is within Areas Subject to Protection
under the Wetlands Protection Act, M.G.L. Ch. 131, § 40, and will result in impacts to Bank, Bordering
Vegetated Wetlands, and Riverfront Area. As demonstrated in the Performance Standards Analysis
prepared by Beals and Thomas submitted with the Notice of Intent, the project has been designed to
comply with the performance standards set forth under 310 CMR 10.54, 10.55, 10.58(4) and will not
have a significant or cumulative adverse effects on the resource areas. Furthermore, the targeted
herbicide treatment to control invasive phragmites will improve vegetative diversity and wildlife habitat
value.
C. The work described is within Areas Subject to Protection under the Town of Hingham Wetlands
Protection Bylaw (Article 22) including Bank, Bordering Vegetated Wetlands, Riverfront Area and Buffer
Zone and the Commission finds the areas are significant to the following wetland values: protection of
public or private water supply, protection of groundwater, flood control, erosion and sedimentation
control, storm damage prevention including coastal storm flowage, prevention of water pollution,
protection of fisheries, protection of shellfish, protection of wildlife and wildlife habitat, protection of
rare species habitat, protection of recreation and open space, protection of aesthetics.
D. As demonstrated in the Supplemental Narrative prepared by Beals and Thomas dated October 18,
2024, the project has been designed to comply with the performance standards set forth under the
Hingham Wetland Regulations (HWR) Sections 19.2, 19.3, 21.1, and will not result in an adverse effect
upon the wetland values of the resource areas. Additionally the proposed work has been found to
comply with the performance standards for work in the Buffer Zone, HWR Section 22.0(d).
E. With incorporation of the special conditions included in this Order, the Commission has determined
the project complies with the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act, M.G.L. Ch. 131 §40 and
Implementing Regulations, 310 CMR 10.00, and the Hingham Wetlands Protection Bylaw and Wetland
Regulations.
Suggested Special Conditions
23. Management of Common Reed (Phragmites australis) shall comply with the approved Invasive
Species Management Plan prepared by Beals and Thomas dated September 19, 2024 and Supplemental
Memorandum prepared by Beals and Thomas dated October 18, 2024 including Table 1: Protocol for
Phragmites Treatment. Any changes to the treatment methods or targeted locations as identified in the
approved Management Plan shall require review and approval of the Conservation Commission prior to
implementation.
24. Prior to commencement of invasive species management work, the applicant shall submit: final
selected herbicide EPA product labels, copy of required license(s) for all herbicide applicators, and the
BRP WM04 License to Apply Herbicides, to the Conservation Office.
25. Phragmites cut material shall be removed in select areas (i.e. open water, stream) if recommended
by the Licensed Herbicide Applicator and wetlands professional. Other cut material is permitted to
remain in place to naturally decompose.

26. Annual reports on the Invasive Species Management Plan prepared by the Licensed Aquatic
Herbicide Applicator (LHA) and/or other qualified professional shall be submitted to the Conservation
Commission detailing methods used (including dates, photos and descriptions of area(s) treated, specific
herbicides used) and results (cover of invasive species) throughout the three (3) year project. The annual
reports shall be submitted no later than December 31st of each year with the first report being
submitted after initial treatment scheduled for September or October 2025. If continued management
of phragmites is recommended by the LHA beyond the 3 year period to achieve restoration goals, an
extension of the OOC shall be requested in accordance with 310 CMR 10.05(8).
27. Upon completion of invasive species management program and prior to issuance of a Certificate of
Compliance, a final report shall be submitted to the Conservation Commission documenting overall
success of invasive species management efforts, including site photos, sketches, etc. of each treatment
area. If, based on results of treatment efforts, revegetation of any resource areas is needed to establish
a native plant community, recommendations shall be provided in the final report.
