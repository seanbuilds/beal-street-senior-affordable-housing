## 1. Overview & Regulatory Jurisdiction

The planned development of **68 units of affordable senior rental housing** at **100 Beal Street** is governed by an intricate intersection of state public housing statutes, disposition controls under M.G.L. c. 121B, and the Chapter 40B Comprehensive Permit regulatory process.

Because 100 Beal Street was acquired and developed under state-aided housing programs (specifically designated as Department of Mental Health / Department of Social Services special needs public housing, Chapter 689 Program, Project 689-01), the fee title of the property is subject to perpetual commonwealth disposition restrictions.

---

## 2. EOHLC / DHCD Title Encumbrance: M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34

### 2.1 The Statutory Restriction
Under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B, Section 34, no municipal housing authority may sell, lease, transfer, or encumber any portion of real property acquired with commonwealth assistance without:
1. Express written authorization and determination of surplus status by the Secretary of EOHLC;
2. Formal review ensuring that the proposed disposition advances the Commonwealth's public housing and affordable housing priorities;
3. Execution of binding land disposition agreements (LDDA) maintaining long-term affordability restrictions.

### 2.2 Recorded Title Encumbrance
Following disputes between the Town of Hingham and the HHA in 2019 regarding an attempted transfer of 19 acres back to municipal control, DHCD intervened and recorded:
- **Instrument:** *Notice of Statutory Transfer Restriction*
- **Registry:** Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, **Book 51379, Page 244** (Recorded July 18, 2019).
- **Substantive Finding:** EOHLC formally confirmed that the HHA cannot alienate School Tract II without state sign-off, effectively reserving the 8.6-acre developable portion for subsidized housing development.

## 3. Chapter 40B Permitting Framework & Project Eligibility (PEL)

### 3.1 Necessity of Chapter 40B
The 100 Beal Street parcel is situated within an **Official and Open Space District (OO)** under the Town of Hingham Zoning By-Law. Multi-family residential structures are prohibited by right and cannot be permitted through municipal special permits. Consequently, the HHA and its selected private development partner, **Peabody Properties**, are advancing the 68-unit development via a **Comprehensive Permit pursuant to M.G.L. c. 40B**.

### 3.2 Project Eligibility Letter (PEL) / Site Approval Requirements
To submit a formal Comprehensive Permit application to the Hingham Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA), Peabody Properties and the HHA must obtain a **Project Eligibility Letter (PEL)** (Site Approval) from an authorized Subsidizing Agency:
- **Eligible Subsidizing Agencies:** MassHousing, EOHLC (under the Local Initiative Program / LIP), or the Massachusetts Housing Partnership (MHP).
- **Prerequisites for PEL Issuance (760 CMR 56.04):**
  1. **Site Control:** Demonstrated through the HHA's fee ownership and the executed Land Disposition and Development Agreement (LDDA) / long-term ground lease with Peabody Properties.
  2. **Applicant Eligibility:** Limited dividend entity qualification or non-profit/public housing authority status.
  3. **Preliminary Financial Feasibility:** Demonstration of fundability through state or federal subsidy sources, including Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC), EOHLC capital subsidies, or MassHousing financing.
  4. **Initial Environmental & Site Review:** Municipal notification and a 30-day municipal comment period, during which the Select Board, Planning Board, and Conservation Commission submit technical concerns to the Subsidizing Agency.

### 3.3 Comparative Beal Street 40B Precedents
Beal Street has been the epicenter of extensive Chapter 40B permitting in Hingham:
- **Broadstone Bare Cove (230 Beal Street / "The Cove"):**
  - Project Eligibility Letter issued by MassHousing on **August 12, 2016** (MassHousing ID: 885).
  - Permitted for 220 units across 5 residential buildings.
  - Constructed adjacent to Bare Cove Park and the MBTA West Hingham commuter rail station.
- **River Stone (Ward Street / French Street):**
  - 36-unit 40B comprehensive permit contested aggressively by the Town of Hingham through the Housing Appeals Committee (HAC), Land Court, and Massachusetts Appeals Court, concluding in final settlement and project completion in November 2025.
- **100 Beal Street (68 Senior Units):**
  - Represents the **fifth major 40B proposal** along the Beal Street corridor.
  - Unlike private commercial 40Bs, 100 Beal Street is sponsored by the municipal housing authority (HHA), creating a distinct regulatory dynamic between the HHA (proponent) and the Town ZBA/Planning Board (reviewing authorities).

## 4. Safe Harbor Analysis: Hingham's Statutory Standing under 760 CMR 56.03

### 4.1 Surpassing the 10% Statutory Minimum
Under M.G.L. c. 40B, § 20 and 760 CMR 56.03, municipalities that have exceeded 10% of their total year-round housing units on the state **Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI)** achieve "Safe Harbor" status:
- Hingham has documented an SHI ratio exceeding 10%, primarily through the addition of large rental developments such as Broadstone Bare Cove and Avalon at Hingham Shipyard.
- **Legal Ramifications:** When a municipality is in Safe Harbor, the local Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) has the legal authority to **deny** a Chapter 40B Comprehensive Permit or impose conditions without risk of automatic reversal by the state Housing Appeals Committee (HAC), provided the ZBA asserts safe harbor within 15 days of opening the public hearing.

### 4.2 Application to 100 Beal Street
- Because 100 Beal Street is sponsored by the HHA and targets seniors earning at or below 30%, 50%, and 60% of AMI, the project is structured as a collaborative municipal-state development.
- However, as community abutters (such as Craftsman Village) have asserted, the Town's Safe Harbor status grants the ZBA legal standing to enforce strict compliance with local wetland bylaws and require scale reductions (e.g., from 68 units down to 46 units) if local environmental impacts on Tucker's Swamp and the Back River ACEC are determined to outweigh local housing needs.
