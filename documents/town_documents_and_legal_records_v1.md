## Executive Overview

The property at **100 Beal Street** is an 8.6-acre developable portion of an overall 15.014-acre parcel owned by the **Hingham Housing Authority (HHA)**. Formally known across historical land records as **"School Tract II"**, the parcel is situated directly adjacent to **Bare Cove Park** and borders the hydrologic watershed of the **Weymouth Back River Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC)**.

The parcel is currently subject to high-profile planning and community deliberation concerning the construction of a **68-unit affordable senior rental housing development** (restricted to seniors aged 62 and older), advanced by the HHA in partnership with selected private developer **Peabody Properties** and engineering/landscape architecture firm **Weston & Sampson**.

This investigative report consolidates the foundational chain of title, municipal agreements, Town Meeting actions, inter-governmental disputes between the Town of Hingham and the HHA/DHCD, state statutory restrictions, procurement records under M.G.L. c. 30B, Chapter 40B comprehensive permitting dynamics, and conservation/environmental adjudications under MassDEP File No. **034-1509**.

## 1. Site Identity, Ownership & Land Registry History

### 1.1 Parcel Identification
- **Address:** 100 Beal Street, Hingham, MA 02043
- **Assessor Record:** Map 58, Block 0, Lot 23 (approx. 15.014 total acres; current senior housing disposition area comprises approx. 8.6 acres).

- **Historical Military Context:** Formerly part of the federal U.S. Naval Ammunition Depot Hingham (operating from 1903 through the mid-20th century before surplus land was ceded to the Town and commonwealth entities, including Bare Cove Park).

   - **Existing Built Improvements:** Built in 1989/1990 under state-aided public housing assistance (6 units of special needs residential group housing, historically known as the Beal Street Residential Program / Bay State Community Services facility).

## 2. The 2001 Memorandum of Understanding & The Town/HHA Legal Impasse

### 2.1 The 2001 Memorandum of Agreement
On **June 12, 2001**, the Town of Hingham and the HHA executed a **Memorandum of Agreement (MOU)** concerning School Tract II / 100 Beal Street, followed by formal correspondence dated **July 31, 2001**. 

- **Substance of the MOU:** The Town had funded and performed significant municipal infrastructure work and site remediation/demolition on School Tract II utilizing local taxpayer capital.
- **The Reciprocal Obligation:** In exchange for the Town's capital expenditures on site improvements, the MOU contemplated that excess, undeveloped portions of School Tract II (approximately 8 to 19 acres outside the immediate footprint of the special needs residential building) would eventually be deeded or transferred back to the Town of Hingham for municipal/conservation/open space utilization.

### 2.2 Impasse, Executive Session, and Litigation Vote (2019)
Over the following 18 years, the contemplated land transfer was never consummated. By early 2019, relations between the Hingham Board of Selectmen (now Select Board) and the HHA deteriorated into a formal legal impasse:

- **Board of Selectmen Executive Session — February 26, 2019:**
  - Convened at 6:45 PM under Purpose 3 of the Open Meeting Law (litigation strategy).
  - Town Real Estate Counsel **Susan Murphy** provided a full brief on the 2001 MOU and the failure of HHA to convey the property.
  - Selectman **Karen Johnson** placed on record that Town taxpayers had directly funded extensive site and demolition work at 100 Beal Street with the explicit legal expectation of property transfer.
  - **Vote:** The Board of Selectmen voted unanimously (3–0) to **file litigation against the Hingham Housing Authority** to compel legal enforcement and specific performance of the 2001 agreement.

### 2.3 The Attempted 2019 Conveyance and DHCD Intervention
Following pressure from the Town's litigation posture:
- **June 5, 2019 Special Meeting of HHA:** The HHA Board of Commissioners voted to approve the transfer of the 19-acre parcel (School Tract II) back to the Town of Hingham.
- **State Intervention (DHCD / M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34):**
  - Upon learning of the pending transfer, the **Massachusetts Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD)**, now the **Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC)**, intervened immediately.
  - DHCD legal counsel issued formal warning letters on **April 19, 2019**, and **July 19, 2019**, declaring the proposed transfer illegal under state housing law.
  - **Statutory Mandate:** Under **M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34**, any parcel acquired or developed with state financial assistance or held under a Contract for Financial Assistance (CFA) is subject to strict state oversight. A local housing authority has no legal power to dispose of, convey, or encumber public housing land without express prior written approval from DHCD/EOHLC and review by the Massachusetts Office of the Attorney General.
  - **Clouding Title / Statutory Notice:** To prevent any unauthorized transfer at the local level, DHCD executed and recorded an encumbrance directly onto the property title:
    - **Instrument:** *Notice of Statutory Transfer Restriction*
    - **Recording Date:** July 18, 2019
    - **Recording Registry:** Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, **Book 51379, Page 244**.
    - **Legal Effect:** Creates a perpetual, blanket statutory title restriction confirming that no conveyance or transfer of School Tract II / 100 Beal Street can occur without formal EOHLC written authorization, effectively blocking the Town's attempt to seize or claim the land.

### 2.4 Formal Rescission by HHA (September 7, 2021)
At the HHA Board of Commissioners meeting on **September 7, 2021**, acting on joint advice from DHCD legal counsel and municipal counsel, the Board formally voted **4–0 to RESCIND** the June 5, 2019 motion that had authorized the transfer to the Town. This solidified the HHA's sole legal ownership and retention of School Tract II for public/affordable housing development.

## 3. Chapter 30B Procurement & Developer RFP

Having reasserted exclusive control over School Tract II, the Hingham Housing Authority initiated steps to activate the surplus land for new affordable housing production.

### 3.1 Board Authorization & Surplus Declaration (September 10, 2024)
At its regular meeting on **September 10, 2024**, the HHA Board of Commissioners voted to:
1. Formally declare approximately 8.6 acres of the 100 Beal Street property as "excess / surplus property" pursuant to public housing modernization and disposition guidelines.
2. Solicit competitive development proposals under the **Massachusetts Uniform Procurement Act (M.G.L. c. 30B, § 16)** governing real property disposition exceeding $35,000 in assessed value.
3. Authorize Executive Director Randy Waters to prepare and issue a competitive **Request for Proposals (RFP)**.

### 3.2 The Request for Proposals (RFP) Specifications
- **RFP Title:** *Request for Proposals for the Development of Affordable Senior Rental Housing at 100 Beal Street, Hingham, MA*
- **Issuance Date:** April 2025
- **Pre-Proposal Meeting & Site Tour:** May 1, 2025
- **Proposal Submission Deadline:** **June 11, 2025, at 4:00 PM EST**
- **Submission Requirements:** 1 original in a 3-ring binder, 5 hard copies, and 1 complete digital file on USB; electronic/fax submissions rejected.
- **Contract Mechanism:** The HHA structured the transaction not as an outright fee simple sale, but as a long-term ground lease under a **Land Disposition and Development Agreement (LDDA)**, ensuring that the fee title remains anchored with the Housing Authority while private development partners finance, construct, operate, and manage the facility.
- **Target Demographics & Units:** 
  - Originally scoped at approximately **60 units** of deeply affordable and mixed-income senior rental apartments (age 62+).
  - Income tiers targeting households earning at or below 30%, 50%, and 60% of Area Median Income (AMI).
- **Award:** Following evaluation by the HHA Selection Committee, the contract was officially awarded to **Peabody Properties**, with site civil engineering and landscape architecture spearheaded by **Weston & Sampson**.

## 4. Chapter 40B Comprehensive Permitting & State Subsidizing Filings

### 4.1 Permitting Route Under M.G.L. c. 40B
Because the property is zoned **Official and Open Space (OO)**, multi-family residential housing is barred by local zoning. Consequently, the HHA and Peabody Properties have chosen to permit the development via a **Comprehensive Permit (Chapter 40B)** before the **Hingham Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA)**:
- Chapter 40B enables the developer to request waivers from local bylaws—including density, dimensional requirements, building height, parking ratios, and local wetland bylaws—provided the project meets health, safety, and environmental standards.
- Under state guidelines, the HHA and Peabody Properties prepared an application for a **Project Eligibility Letter (PEL)** from the subsidizing agency (such as **MassHousing**, **EOHLC**, or the **Massachusetts Housing Partnership / MHP**).

### 4.2 Expansion to 68 Units & Community Pushback (2026)
Between late 2025 and mid-2026, Peabody Properties refined the architectural and site civil designs with Weston & Sampson, expanding the planned development from the RFP baseline of 60 units to **68 units**:
- **Structure:** A proposed 3-story residential structure designed to minimize footprint impact by nestling into existing woodland topography.
- **Local Controversy:** 
  - In August 2026, community debate intensified over the scale of the 68-unit proposal.
  - A prominent opinion editorial published in the *Hingham Anchor* on August 18, 2026 (authored by resident Shannon Brinkley) questioned the expansion from early historical concepts (20–48 units) to 68 units on land zoned Official and Open Space.
  - Concerns were raised regarding the potential use of 40B waivers to override local wetland protection buffer standards, especially given that Hingham has already surpassed the statutory 10% Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI) threshold (the safe harbor benchmark under 760 CMR 56.03). Under safe harbor, the ZBA possesses greater statutory latitude to deny or condition 40B applications unless the subsidizing agency or housing authority reaches an accord with the municipality.

## 5. Environmental Adjudications & Physical Site Constraints

The 100 Beal Street parcel is one of the most environmentally sensitive development sites in Hingham due to three intersecting ecological boundaries:
1. **Weymouth Back River Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC):** Portions of the southern and eastern sectors of the parcel drain into tributaries and wetland systems designated as **Outstanding Resource Waters (ORW)** tributary to the Back River ACEC.
2. **Bare Cove Park & Wildlife Refuge:** Directly borders the northern and western margins of Bare Cove Park, an environmentally protected municipal sanctuary home to sensitive bird nesting habitats and vernal pools.
3. **Internal Wetland Systems & Hydrology:** A substantial portion of the parcel consists of poorly drained soils, high groundwater tables, and forested wetlands.

### 5.1 Conservation Commission ANRAD / ORAD Review (MassDEP No. 034-1509)
To legally fix and confirm wetland resource boundaries prior to filing formal Chapter 40B site designs, the applicant team retained **Lucas Environmental, LLC** (represented by environmental scientist Christopher Lucas) and **Control Point Associates, Inc.** to conduct exhaustive site delineations:

1. **ANRAD Filing:**
   - **MassDEP File Number:** **034-1509**
   - **Applicant Representative:** Christopher Lucas, Lucas Environmental, LLC
   - **Civil Delineation Plan:** Prepared by Control Point Associates, Inc., dated September 9, 2024.
   - **Resource Areas Identified:**
     - Three separate systems of **Bordering Vegetative Wetlands (BVW)** subject to jurisdiction under M.G.L. c. 131, § 40 and 310 CMR 10.55.
     - Two systems of **Isolated Vegetative Wetlands (IVW)** protected under the Town of Hingham Wetlands Protection By-Law.
     - A verified **Vernal Pool** located in the southwestern quadrant of the parcel, triggering critical 100-foot no-disturbance and buffer zone protections under local bylaws.
     - 200-foot **Riverfront Area** and Mean Annual High Water (MAHW) lines associated with ACEC-tributary streams.

2. **Order of Resource Area Delineation (ORAD):**
   - **Public Hearing:** Opened and concluded on **November 4, 2024**, before the Hingham Conservation Commission.
   - **Vote:** The Conservation Commission voted **5–0** to approve and issue a comprehensive Order of Resource Area Delineation (issued on **November 7, 2024**).
   - **Significance:** The ORAD validates all boundary flags on the Control Point Associates plan for three years, binding any future Notice of Intent (NOI) or Chapter 40B site plan review to these exact wetland boundaries.

## 7. Conclusions and Key Strategic Insights

1. **Clear Fee Ownership with State Protection:** 
   Although the Town of Hingham attempted to claim School Tract II based on the 2001 MOU and taxpayer-funded demolition work, the intervention of DHCD/EOHLC and the recording of the **Notice of Statutory Transfer Restriction at Book 51379, Page 244** confirms that fee title remains legally unassailable with the Hingham Housing Authority. The Town cannot compel a unilateral transfer of this property.

2. **Zoning Barrier and Chapter 40B Necessity:** 
   Because the parcel is zoned Official and Open Space (OO), the development cannot proceed through standard municipal special permits or site plan review. Chapter 40B is legally mandatory to bypass the OO zoning prohibition.

3. **Safe Harbor Vulnerability:** 
   Because Hingham currently meets the 10% Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI) threshold, the Hingham Zoning Board of Appeals is not statutorily compelled to grant wholesale zoning waivers if it determines that local health, environmental, or open space concerns outweigh the regional need for affordable housing. This heightens the importance of community negotiations between Peabody Properties, the HHA, and the Town.

4. **Rigid Wetland and ACEC Envelope:** 
   With MassDEP File No. **034-1509** fully adjudicated and the ORAD issued on November 7, 2024, the developable footprint of the 8.6-acre parcel is strictly constrained by confirmed Bordering Vegetative Wetlands, Isolated Vegetative Wetlands, a vernal pool, and the adjacent Weymouth Back River ACEC. The three-story, 68-unit site plan engineered by Weston & Sampson must navigate these setbacks or seek specific bylaw waivers through the 40B comprehensive permit.
