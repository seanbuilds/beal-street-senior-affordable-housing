Subject: Ensuring Responsible Stewardship of Hingham’s Natural Resources

Dear Members of the Hingham Conservation Commission,
While recreational opportunities are important, they must be balanced with the responsibility to protect Hingham’s
natural environment for stewardship and for future generations.
This proposal to build 8 pickleball courts raises concerns due to its location near wetlands that feed the already
stressed Back River, as well as its proximity to an Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC). Notably, the
ACEC boundary is not clearly identified in the proposal materials, making it difficult to assess impacts.
The site also supports many migratory bird species identified by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, many of which
nest nearby. The noise from pickleball could disrupt these habitats, and seasonal restrictions should be required.
Encroachment into buffer zones, including within the 50-foot buffer (no matter how small) and the 200-foot Riverfront,
wastes an opportunity to provide these sensitive natural resources with the space to recover, flourish and avoid longterm environmental harm. While the reduction in impervious area is helpful, the Town should lead by adhering strictly
and demonstrating commitment to its bylaws.
The ongoing and continuously encroaching developments in the area has already impacted these wetlands. Adding
further pressure, especially at the scale of eight courts—exceeding Hingham’s typical maximum of six—raises
serious concerns.
If this project proceeds, it should include strong safeguards: no salt, fertilizer, or chemical use, and landscaping that
restores natural vegetation rather than introducing grass areas.
The decisions made today will shape the health of these ecosystems for years to come. I urge the Commission to
act with caution and prioritize environmental preservation.
Thank you for your consideration.
Sincerely,
Hilary Aborn

Proposed Pickleball courts on Back River

Subject: Request to Modify Proposed Pickleball Courts Near Sensitive Wetlands
Dear Members of the Hingham Conservation Commission,
I am writing to respectfully urge the Commission to significantly modify the Recreation Department’s
proposal to construct eight pickleball courts in an environmentally sensitive area.
The proposal relies on a reduction in impervious surface from demolition of a former naval
ammunition structure to justify encroachment into wetland buffer zones. This tradeoff should not
come at the expense of established protections. The Town should demonstrate environmental
stewardship by fully honoring its own wetland bylaws.
The plan for 8 pickleball courts includes development within the 50-foot buffer in contravention of
Bylaw 23.2 (irrespective of how small; it still occurs), and does not adequately respect the 200-foot
Riverfront boundary. This is particularly concerning given that the river is an Outstanding Resource
Water feeding the Back River Estuary.
These wetlands support the Back River, which is already polluted and undergoing rehabilitation.
Additional encroachment will only hinder recovery. The adjacent Back River Area of Critical
Environmental Concern (ACEC), whose boundary does not seem to be clearly or accurately
demarcated in the NOI materials, underscores the ecological sensitivity of this site.
The U.S. Fish & Wildlife Serviceidentifies 23 migratory bird species in this area, many of which nest
nearby. The noise associated with pickleball raises serious concerns, and play should be prohibited
during nesting seasons.
I also urge the Commission to prohibit the use of salt, fertilizers, and chemical treatments such as
mosquito spraying, as a condition to any order due to the proximity to sensitive natural resources.
Landscaping should favor natural vegetation rather than maintained grass areas, particularly to the
north and west.
Finally, the scale of this proposal is excessive. Nowhere else in Hingham are eight courts
constructed—six courts has been the maximum and should remain the standard.
Given cumulative environmental impacts already observed in the area, including harm to wetlands
and vernal pools, this proposal warrants strong reconsideration.
Sincerely,
Steve Cobble

Subject: Concerns Regarding Compliance with Wetland and ACEC Protections

Dear Conservation Commission,
I am writing to raise concerns regarding the 8 proposed pickleball courts and their compliance with the spirit of
Hingham’s wetland bylaws and environmental protections.
The proposal, irrespective of how small, includes development within the 50-foot buffer, which conflicts with
Bylaw 23.2, and does not appear to fully respect the 200-foot Riverfront boundary associated with an
Outstanding Resource Water.
The rationale of reduced impervious surface does not justify these encroachments. Upholding bylaw
requirements is essential to maintaining regulatory integrity.
The wetlands feed and support the Back River, an impaired system undergoing restoration. Additional
encroachment could undermine these efforts. The riverfront in the vicinity of the development is associated
with Hockley Run, which is an essential part of the ecosystem and feeds the fish run associated with the Back
River. That fish in turn are food for the many fish further out to sea on which we depend for food.
Furthermore, the project area lies adjacent to an Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC), yet its
boundary is not clearly demarcated in the application materials. This lack of clarity raises concerns about
whether impacts have been fully assessed. The ACEC nomination, which was unanimously brought by the
Town’s Select Board, Planning Board, the Conservation Commission and the Bare Cove Park Committee,
referred to the many migratory birds in the area and that this habitat was worthy of the utmost protection for
wildlife. Noise impacts from pickleball are completely out of character with that protection and should be
addressed, including prohibiting play during nesting seasons.
I also advocate strict prohibitions on salt, fertilizer, and chemical use, and a requirement for naturalized
landscaping, as a condition of any development in the area.
Finally, the proposed scale of eight courts exceeds Hingham’s norm of six courts and should be reduced
accordingly.
Our founder, Mary F. Toomey, with the support of Dr. Mary Sears of the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution
worked tirelessly in the sixties and seventies to preserve the Back River and the surrounding land. It’s time for
Hingham to embrace their legacy and work to protect this valuable resource rather than destroy it.
I urge the Commission to ensure full compliance and appropriate protections.
Respectfully,
Linda DiAngelo
President, Back River Watershed Association

Dear Members of the Select Board and Conservation Commission,
I am writing in response to the letters and in-person concerns raised at your recent meetings
regarding the Pickleball Project approved at the 2025 Town Meeting.
Some critics have suggested that the late Mary Toomey—former Chair of the Back River
Committee of Weymouth and Hingham and a respected environmental advocate—would have
opposed this project. While Mary was deeply committed to protecting natural resources, her
vision centered on preserving the river and estuary from contamination, providing safe habitat
for wildlife, and creating parkland that is accessible to the public.
I had the privilege of knowing Mary and working alongside her in advocating for the cleanup of
Jay Cashman’s property on Route 3A in North Weymouth. At the time, the rear of that property
had become an illegal dumping ground, as documented in a Patriot Ledger article dated April
1994. Our efforts, along with those of others, ultimately contributed to the creation of Abigail
Adams State Park, which was dedicated in 2003. Mary was later honored with a plaque at the
site—a ceremony I was proud to attend as her friend and supporter.
In that spirit, I believe Mary would recognize the value of the approved pickleball project. The
Recreation Commission is taking the lead in removing the Tracers Building, a condemned
building. We will be cleaning up the site which currently has pipes, cement blocks and other
debris strewn aside it.
Importantly, the project has been designed to avoid impacts to the wetlands and the nearby
vernal pool. The engineering plans clearly outline measures to meet all environmental
requirements.
This project represents a meaningful opportunity to remove a condemned building and protect
the natural resources Mary Toomey worked so hard to preserve.
Thank you for your time, consideration, and continued service to our community.
Sincerely,
Vicki Donlan
Chair, Hingham Recreation Commission
