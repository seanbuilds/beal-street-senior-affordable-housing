HINGHAM AFFORDABLE HOUSING TRUST
HINGHAM, MASSACHUSETTS
MEETING MINUTES

Date: September 20, 2017
Location: South Hearing Room, Hingham Town Hall
Called to Order: 7:05 pm
Attendance: Tim White, Chairman; Brittan DuBose, Anita Comerford, Nancy Kerber, Mike MacDonald,
Al Chambers, Kathleen Amonte
Approval of Minutes from Meeting of August 16, 2017: Voted – a motion was made by Kathleen
Amonte and seconded by Anita Comerford to approve the Open Session minutes from August 16, 2017.
A vote was taken and all were in favor; the motion passed.
Treasurer’s Report/Approval of Invoice: Anita went over the report. The September report will show
the $150,000 payment for Rust House. Anita and Sue Nickerson were wondering if they could account
for Lincoln School separately. Tim does not see an issue and thinks it would clarify things.
Voted – a motion was made by Nancy Kerber and seconded by Brittan DuBose to approve the NAHRO
invoice of $75. A vote was taken and all were in favor; the motion passed.
Voted – a motion was made by Al Chambers and seconded by Nancy Kerber to approve a bill from
GateHouse Media for the advertising of the RFP for Whiting Street. A vote was taken and all were in
favor; the motion passed.
Voted – a motion was made by Al Chambers and seconded by Nancy Kerber to approve a bill from
MacKenzie for 10 Cedar Street work. A vote was taken and all were in favor; the motion passed.
Voted – a motion was made by Al Chambers and seconded by Brittan DuBose to approve the treasurers
report. A vote was taken and all were in favor; the motion passed.
Whiting Street: There was one response to the RFP, Habitat for Humanity. Carol Costello was kind
enough to put together an evaluation form. Trust members took time to evaluate the proposal
individually. Before they did that, the Board agreed to skip to Beal Street for a few minutes since there
was a resident there who wanted to speak to the Trust.
80 Beal Street: Shannon Brinkley attended. She owns one of the affordable units and is concerned with
the costs that have come up. Her condo fee has doubled and she was assessed $4375 for snow removal
from two years ago. She felt there was very short notice and felt threatened to pay. Her concern is that
she will not be able to stay there. Tim does not believe there is a cap on her condo fees. The Trust finds
the fact that the condo fees have gone up so quickly troubling.
Al mentions that the condo association should consider making it a town road so the town would plow
it. Tim will follow up with the condo association before the next meeting and will also reach out to
DHCD.

Whiting Street (Resumed): Tim collects the evaluation forms and the results are tallied. The proposal is
clearly met with approval from the Trust. The next procedure is to vote and send notice to Habitat.
Then there will be a negotiation procedure and everyone will come up with a schedule. Mike had a
question on funding, which was discussed.
Voted – A motion was made by Mike MacDonald and seconded by Nancy Kerber - Will the Hingham
Affordable Housing Trust (the “Trust”) designate South Shore Habitat for Humanity (the ”Habitat”), 20
Mathewson Drive, Weymouth, MA 02189, as the Designated Developer of the property known as 302304 Whiting Street, Hingham, MA 02043, subject to certain conditions and terms outlined in the Whiting
Street Property Request for Proposals (the “RFP”), due on September 13, 2017, and the Habitat
Response to the RFP? A vote was taken and all were in favor; the motion passed.
Rust House: The purchase closed last week. A deed rider has been drafted that is the standard
affordability rider. Now Father Bill’s is working to get the property on the town’s affordability inventory.
Old Ship: Tim has been contacted by Old Ship. The developer is out of the picture. The church would
like to set up a meeting with folks from the Town and would like Tim to attend.
Selectmen’s Parcel: Nancy had a good meeting with Sharon Napier from the Hingham Housing
Authority about 100 Beal Street. They are proposing a mix of affordable apartments for 55+. Right now
there is a deed restriction on the property that prevents them from doing that which expires in March of
2018. The Housing Authority did a needs assessment study. They would like to have a local and veteran
preference. Tim and Nancy will try to attend their next meeting.
The Trust is asked to think about what they can do to maintain affordable units in Town.
Lincoln School Apartments Update: Aylene was sick and could not attend. She gave an update via
email.
On the elevator project: still some back and forth going on with the Town, the RFP should go out by end
of September for work to start in April, the Town had asked the consultant to make some changes in his
proposed RFP and all have been made.
Other improvements underway include new outdoor benches which should be in early October and also
new outdoor lights which will be done 1/2 in 2017 and 1/2 in 2018.
There is a new onsite maintenance manager in place, named Ryan, the fellow that had been there for
years retired. The residents couldn't say enough positive about the new fellow and seem to love him.
The Board asked Ryan to do a "review" of the property as a fresh set of eyes and plan a walk around
with him on 10/3 at 8 am if anyone wants to join us.
Emily Wentworth and Susan Murphy reviewed the Corcoran Management contract at the Board's
request and are doing a revised version with changes they think we need to have in place.
