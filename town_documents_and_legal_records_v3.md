## Executive Overview

The property at **100 Beal Street** is an 8.6-acre disposition parcel (**Lot B**) carved out from an overall 15.014-acre parcel owned in fee simple by the **Hingham Housing Authority (HHA)**. Formally known across historical municipal and land records as **"School Tract II"**, the parcel is situated directly adjacent to **Bare Cove Park** (484 acres) and borders the hydrologic watershed of the **Weymouth Back River Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC)**.

The parcel is currently subject to planning and community deliberation concerning the construction of a **68-unit affordable senior rental housing community** (restricted to seniors aged 62 and older), advanced by the HHA in partnership with selected private developer **Peabody Properties** (AHSC Developers LLC) under a proposed **99-year triple-net ground lease**.

This investigative report consolidates the foundational chain of title, municipal agreements, Town Meeting actions, inter-governmental disputes between the Town of Hingham and the HHA/DHCD, state statutory restrictions under M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34, procurement records under M.G.L. c. 30B, Chapter 40B comprehensive permitting dynamics, and conservation/environmental adjudications under MassDEP File No. **034-1509**.

## 2. The 2001 Memorandum of Understanding & The Town/HHA Legal Impasse

### 2.1 The 2001 Memorandum of Agreement
On **June 12, 2001**, the Town of Hingham and the HHA executed a **Memorandum of Agreement (MOU)** concerning School Tract II / 100 Beal Street, followed by formal correspondence dated **July 31, 2001**. 
- **Substance of the MOU:** The Town funded and performed significant municipal infrastructure work and site remediation/demolition on School Tract II, demolishing underground military ammunition bunkers.
- **The Contemplated Disposition:** In exchange for the Town's capital expenditures on bunker removal, the MOU contemplated that excess, undeveloped portions of School Tract II would eventually be deeded or transferred back to the Town of Hingham for municipal open space utilization.

### 2.2 Impasse, Executive Session, and Litigation Vote (February 26, 2019)
Over the following 18 years, the contemplated land transfer was never consummated. By early 2019, relations between the Hingham Board of Selectmen (now Select Board) and the HHA reached a formal legal impasse:
- **Board of Selectmen Executive Session & Open Vote — February 26, 2019:**
  - Convened under Purpose 3 of the Open Meeting Law (litigation strategy).
  - Town Real Estate Counsel **Susan Murphy** briefed the Board on the 2001 MOU and HHA's failure to convey the property.
  - Selectman **Karen Johnson** placed on record that Town taxpayers had directly funded extensive site and demolition work at 100 Beal Street with the explicit legal expectation of property transfer.
  - **Vote:** In open session following executive session, the Board of Selectmen voted unanimously (**3–0**) to **authorize litigation against the Hingham Housing Authority** to enforce the 2001 agreement.

### 2.3 The Attempted 2019 Conveyance and State Intervention (M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34)
Following pressure from the Town's litigation posture:
- **June 5, 2019 Special Meeting of HHA:** The HHA Board of Commissioners voted to approve the transfer of School Tract II back to the Town of Hingham.
- **State Intervention (DHCD / EOHLC):**
  - Upon learning of the pending transfer, the **Massachusetts Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD)**, now the **Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC)**, intervened immediately.
  - DHCD legal counsel issued formal warning letters on **April 19, 2019**, and **July 19, 2019**, declaring the proposed transfer illegal under state housing law.
  - **Statutory Mandate:** Under **M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34**, any parcel acquired or developed with state financial assistance or held under a Contract for Financial Assistance (CFA) is subject to strict state oversight. A local housing authority has no legal power to dispose of, convey, or encumber public housing land without express prior written approval from DHCD/EOHLC.
  - **Clouding Title / Statutory Notice:** To prevent any unauthorized transfer, DHCD recorded an encumbrance directly onto the property title:
    - **Instrument:** *Notice of Statutory Transfer Restriction*
    - **Recording Date:** July 18, 2019
    - **Recording Registry:** Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, **Book 51379, Page 244**.
    - **Legal Effect:** Creates an enforceable statutory title restriction confirming that no conveyance or transfer of School Tract II / 100 Beal Street can occur without formal EOHLC written authorization, effectively blocking the Town's attempt to reclaim the land.

### 2.4 Formal Rescission by HHA (September 7, 2021)
At the HHA Board of Commissioners meeting on **September 7, 2021**, acting on joint advice from DHCD legal counsel and municipal counsel, the Board formally voted **4–0 to RESCIND** the June 5, 2019 motion that had authorized the transfer to the Town. This solidified the HHA's sole legal ownership and retention of School Tract II for public affordable housing development.

## 3. Disentangling Municipal Projects: Center for Active Living (CAL) vs. 100 Beal Street

A critical legal and factual distinction exists between two separate Hingham projects that have occasionally been conflated in public discourse due to their shared proximity to Bare Cove Park and the Weymouth Back River watershed:

1. **Center for Active Living (HCAL) — Municipal Senior Center Facility:**
   - **Sponsor:** Town of Hingham / Select Board / Center for Active Living Building Committee.
   - **Proposed Site:** Bare Cove Park Drive (within Bare Cove Park municipal land).
   - **Purpose:** Construction of a new municipal senior community center and Council on Aging recreational/social service building.
   - **Town Meeting Actions:**
     - **2025 Annual Town Meeting (Article 15):** Preliminary funding and pre-construction design authorizations.
     - **April 27, 2026 Annual Town Meeting (Article 12):** A **$29.93M debt-exclusion borrowing request** to fund construction of the new senior community center at Bare Cove Park Drive.
     - **Vote Outcome:** The article failed to achieve the statutory two-thirds majority required for municipal borrowing (**510 in favor, 470 opposed**).
   - **Legal Reality:** Article 12 was strictly a municipal capital borrowing request and **not a "land swap" proposal**. 100 Beal Street was never an alternative site for the municipal senior center building.

2. **100 Beal Street — HHA Senior Affordable Rental Housing:**
   - **Sponsor:** Hingham Housing Authority (HHA), an independent public body corporate and politic.
   - **Site:** 100 Beal Street (Lot B, 8.6 acres).
   - **Purpose:** Construction and operation of a 68-unit, 100% affordable senior residential apartment community (restricted to seniors earning 30%, 50%, and 60% of AMI).
   - **Legal Reality:** The failure of the Bare Cove Park senior center debt authorization in April 2026 did not "solidify" 100 Beal Street for housing. The HHA had already declared the land surplus, issued its RFP, and awarded the residential development rights to Peabody Properties months earlier (in August 2025). The two projects are completely separate in governance, financing, and legal ownership.

## 4. Chapter 30B Procurement, Developer Selection & 99-Year Ground Lease

### 4.1 Board Authorization & Surplus Declaration (September 10, 2024)
At its regular meeting on **September 10, 2024**, the HHA Board of Commissioners voted to:
1. Formally declare approximately 8.6 acres of the 100 Beal Street property (**Lot B**) as "excess / surplus property" pursuant to state housing modernization and disposition guidelines.
2. Solicit competitive development proposals under the **Massachusetts Uniform Procurement Act (M.G.L. c. 30B, § 16)** governing real property disposition.
3. Authorize Executive Director Randy Waters to prepare and issue a competitive **Request for Proposals (RFP)**.

### 4.2 Engineering & Due Diligence of Record for the RFP Package
The technical due diligence, survey, and civil layout components for the HHA RFP package were prepared by specialized engineering consultants:
- **Bohler Engineering:** Prepared the site civil feasibility study, ANR subdivision layout (Lots A, B, and C), and due diligence reporting incorporated into the RFP.
- **Control Point Associates, Inc. (CPA):** Conducted the comprehensive ALTA/NSPS land title survey, topographic mapping, and instrument-located boundary surveys.
- **Lucas Environmental, LLC:** Performed professional wetland delineation, environmental resource analysis, and Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act filings.
- **Nangle Engineering:** Conducted electrical and mechanical site utility reviews.

### 4.3 Request for Proposals (RFP) Specifications & Award
- **RFP Title:** *Request for Proposals for the Development of Affordable Senior Rental Housing at 100 Beal Street, Hingham, MA*
- **Issuance Date:** April 16, 2025
- **Pre-Proposal Meeting & Site Tour:** May 1, 2025
- **Submission Deadline:** June 11, 2025
- **Target Unit Count:** RFP sought proposals for approximately 60 senior affordable rental units (expanding to 68 units under Peabody Properties).
- **Contract Mechanism:** A **99-year triple-net (NNN) ground lease** under a **Land Disposition and Development Agreement (LDDA)**:
  - HHA retains permanent fee simple ownership of the land.
  - Developer/tenant (AHSC Peabody Developers LLC) is solely responsible for 100% of construction, taxes/PILOT, maintenance, insurance, and operating expenses.
  - Upfront consideration: Minimum $500,000 initial ground lease payment + ongoing annual rent.
  - **100% Public Reversion:** Upon expiration of the 99-year term, full title to the building, grounds, and all improvements surrenders and reverts 100% to the HHA at zero acquisition cost to the public.
- **Developer Award:** On **August 12, 2025**, the HHA Board of Commissioners voted unanimously (**5–0**) to award the development contract to **Peabody Properties / Affordable Housing Services Corp (AHSC)**. Formal LDDA approval was executed on **August 25, 2026**.

## 5. Chapter 40B Comprehensive Permitting & Safe Harbor Dynamics

### 5.1 Permitting Route Under M.G.L. c. 40B
Because the property is zoned **Official and Open Space (OO)**, multi-family residential housing is barred by local zoning. Consequently, the development is proceeding via a **Comprehensive Permit (Chapter 40B)** before the **Hingham Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA)**:
- Chapter 40B enables the developer to request waivers from local zoning bylaws—including dimensional requirements, building height, parking ratios, and local wetland bylaws—provided the project meets state health, safety, and environmental standards.
- Under state guidelines, the applicant team prepared an application for a **Project Eligibility Letter (PEL)** from the subsidizing agency (MassHousing / EOHLC / MHP).

### 5.2 Unit Scope Expansion (68 Units) & Statutory Safe Harbor
- **Scope:** 3-story residential structure comprising **68 units** (restricted to seniors earning 30%, 50%, and 60% of AMI). The expansion from earlier preliminary concepts (20–48 units) and the RFP baseline (60 units) to 68 units optimizes Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) and state subsidy equity.
- **Statutory Safe Harbor:** Under **760 CMR 56.03**, the Town of Hingham holds statutory "safe harbor" because its Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI) currently exceeds the 10% threshold. Under safe harbor, the ZBA has statutory discretion to deny an unfriendly 40B or impose local conditions without state override. Consequently, this project proceeds as a **negotiated "friendly" Chapter 40B** with municipal stakeholder alignment.
