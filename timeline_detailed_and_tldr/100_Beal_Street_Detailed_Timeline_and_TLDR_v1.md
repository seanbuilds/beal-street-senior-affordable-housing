## The Executive TLDR: The Whole Story in 60 Seconds

> **TLDR: THE BOTTOM LINE IN PLAIN ENGLISH**
>
> * **The Land:** In 1989, Hingham sold 15 acres of former Navy depot land off Beal Street to the independent Hingham Housing Authority for $45,751 to build a group home for troubled youth. A 12-bed home was built on 2 acres and has operated quietly for decades.
> * **The Senior Housing Plan:** The remaining 13 acres were cleaned up of old WWII ammo bunkers in 2001. In 2006, Hingham Town Meeting voted by a 2/3 supermajority to allow senior affordable housing on the empty 8-acre plateau. But Town Hall never filed the deed paperwork at the Registry of Deeds.
> * **The 2019 Town Hall Lawsuit Threat:** In 2019, right before the 30-year deed mark, the Select Board voted 3–0 to sue the Housing Authority to take the land back. Under pressure, the Housing Authority initially agreed to hand it over.
> * **The State Steps In:** The Commonwealth of Massachusetts (DHCD) intervened and said **NO**. Because state public housing money was used, state law (M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34) strictly forbids giving away housing authority land. The State recorded a legal restriction at the Registry of Deeds blocking any transfer, and the Housing Authority voted 4–0 to cancel the giveaway.
> * **The Peabody Project:** With ownership secure, the Housing Authority issued a 155-page bid in 2025 and awarded a 99-year ground lease to Peabody Properties to build a 3-story, 68-unit affordable senior community. The developer pays 100% of construction and gives $500,000 to the town housing authority.
> * **The Big Town Meeting Rumor Cleared Up:** Town Meeting **NEVER** killed 100 Beal Street. The project rejected on April 27, 2026 was the **Center for Active Living (CAL)**—a separate $29.93 million municipal building inside Bare Cove Park that would have raised property taxes. 100 Beal Street costs taxpayers $0 in town borrowing and remains an active, permitted housing development.

### Milestone 1: April 21, 1989 – Town Conveys School Tract II to HHA
* **Document Date:** March 7, 1989 | **Recording Date:** April 21, 1989  
* **Recording Citation:** Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, Book 09097, Page 158; Document No. 29758  
* **Primary Parties:** Inhabitants of the Town of Hingham (Grantor) &rarr; Hingham Housing Authority (Grantee)  
* **Status of Proof:** Recorded Legal Instrument  
* **Primary Repository File:** [`downloaded_sources/documents/Hingham_Beal_Street_Deed_1989_and_ZBA_Decision.pdf`](file:///Volumes/BLK4TB/Housing%20Topics/Beal%20Street%20Senior%20Affordable%20Housing/downloaded_sources/documents/Hingham_Beal_Street_Deed_1989_and_ZBA_Decision.pdf)

> **TLDR: What This Milestone Means in Plain English**  
> Thirty-seven years ago, Hingham sold 15 acres of former military land off Beal Street to the town's Housing Authority for $45,751. The town included a rule: build and run a home for troubled teenagers. If you stop running the youth home, the town has the right to step back in and take the land back—but the deed explicitly stated this right would expire after 30 years.

#### Comprehensive Documentary Details
1. **Conveyance & Consideration:** The Town of Hingham conveyed a 15.014-acre parcel designated as "School Tract II" to the Hingham Housing Authority for a stated consideration of \$45,751.
2. **Specific Use Restriction (Page 159):** The deed stipulated that the premises were conveyed *"solely for the purpose of constructing, operating and maintaining an adolescent residential educational facility, group care facility or other similar facility for troubled adolescents."*
3. **The 30-Year Reverter Provision (Page 160):** The deed provided that if the premises ceased to be used for adolescent care, the grantor Town had the right to enter and terminate the estate:
   > *"provided, however, that said right of entry shall terminate after thirty years from the date hereof as provided in Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 184A, Section 3."*
4. **Historical Legal Context:** Under Massachusetts property law, rights of entry and possibilities of reverter are strictly limited by statutory duration limits (M.G.L. c. 184A, § 3 as codified in 1989, and M.G.L. c. 184, § 23) to prevent ancient restrictions from clouding title indefinitely.

### Milestone 7: April–June 2019 – State DHCD Intervenes to Protect Public Housing Land
* **Key Dates:** April 19, 2019 & June 26, 2019  
* **State Agency:** Massachusetts Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD, now EOHLC)  
* **State Official:** Amy Stitely, Associate Director of Public Housing  
* **Governing Statute:** Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 121B, Section 34  
* **Status of Proof:** State Administrative Supervisory Letters  
* **Primary Repository File:** [`downloaded_sources/meeting_records/HHA_Minutes_2019-07-09_100_Beal_School_Tract_II.pdf`](file:///Volumes/BLK4TB/Housing%20Topics/Beal%20Street%20Senior%20Affordable%20Housing/downloaded_sources/meeting_records/HHA_Minutes_2019-07-09_100_Beal_School_Tract_II.pdf)

> **TLDR: What This Milestone Means in Plain English**  
> The Commonwealth of Massachusetts stepped in and said: **"Stop."** Local housing authorities are independent state corporations, not Town departments. Because state money was used to build public housing on Beal Street, state law strictly prohibits giving away housing land without written state approval. The state warned that Hingham Town Hall had no legal right to force a land transfer.

#### Comprehensive Documentary Details
1. **Independent Statutory Entity:** Under M.G.L. c. 121B, a local housing authority is an independent public body corporate and politic. It is not an arm of municipal government and its assets cannot be seized or surrendered to settle municipal disputes.
2. **State Alienation Restriction (M.G.L. c. 121B, § 34):** The statute explicitly dictates that no housing authority real estate acquired or developed with state assistance may be sold, transferred, or alienated without prior written approval from the state department.
3. **State Warnings to HHA & Town:** DHCD notified both the Housing Authority and Town Counsel that the proposed transfer violated state housing policy and would not receive Commonwealth approval.

> **TLDR: What This Milestone Means in Plain English**

* **Meeting Date:** April 27, 2026  
* **Legislative Body:** Hingham Annual Town Meeting (Warrant Article 12)  
* **Subject Matter:** \$29,930,000 Debt Exclusion Borrowing for Center for Active Living (CAL) at Bare Cove Park  
* **Vote Result:** 510 Yes, 470 No (52.0% in favor; Failed 2/3 Debt Supermajority)  
* **Status of Proof:** Certified Town Meeting Legislative Record  
* **Primary Repository File:** [`downloaded_sources/documents/Hingham_Town_Meeting_Warrant_2026.pdf`](file:///Volumes/BLK4TB/Housing%20Topics/Beal%20Street%20Senior%20Affordable%20Housing/downloaded_sources/documents/Hingham_Town_Meeting_Warrant_2026.pdf) & [`agent_created_deliverables/executive_reports/Center_for_Active_Living_CAL_Comprehensive_Report_v1.pdf`](file:///Volumes/BLK4TB/Housing%20Topics/Beal%20Street%20Senior%20Affordable%20Housing/agent_created_deliverables/executive_reports/Center_for_Active_Living_CAL_Comprehensive_Report_v1.pdf)

> **TLDR: What This Milestone Means in Plain English**  
> **This is where all the rumors come from!** Town Meeting did NOT vote down 100 Beal Street. Voters rejected a completely different proposal: the Select Board wanted to borrow $30 million in taxpayer debt to build the "Center for Active Living" (a daytime municipal senior center) inside Bare Cove Park. Under state finance law, town borrowing requires a 2/3 supermajority (66.7%). It only got 52%, so the $30 million bond failed. 100 Beal Street was never on the ballot!

#### Comprehensive Documentary Details
1. **The Article 12 Proposal:** The Select Board sponsored Warrant Article 12 asking taxpayers to authorize \$29.93 million in general obligation borrowing to construct a municipal daytime senior center on Bare Cove Park Drive.
2. **Statutory Supermajority Requirement (M.G.L. c. 44, § 7):** Because it proposed municipal debt borrowing, state law strictly mandated a two-thirds supermajority (66.7%).
3. **The Vote Tally:** The vote was 510 in favor, 470 opposed (52.0%). While a simple majority supported it, it fell far short of the required two-thirds supermajority and was declared defeated.
4. **The Confusion:** Because both projects served seniors and both were situated near Bare Cove Park / Tucker's Swamp, many residents mistakenly believed Town Meeting had "killed the senior housing project." In reality, 100 Beal Street was never voted on.
